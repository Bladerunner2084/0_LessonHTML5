The problem nothing else catches

You establish something important early. Two hundred pages later you write a scene that only works if the reader is still holding it. To you it is obvious — you wrote it, you have reread it forty times. To them it was a single sentence, a long time ago.

Your editor comes back with:

“I didn’t follow that scene.”

And you think: but I explained it in chapter four. You are both right.

Every other continuity tool treats reader knowledge as permanent — revealed once, known forever. Readers do not work that way. This one models forgetting.
